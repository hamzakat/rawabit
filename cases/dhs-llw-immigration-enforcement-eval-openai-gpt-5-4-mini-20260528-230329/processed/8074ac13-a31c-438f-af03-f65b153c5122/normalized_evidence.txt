Children in Chicago’s Old Irving Park neighbourhood were preparing for a Halloween parade on Oct. 25 when federal agents reportedly deployed tear gas on the street to disperse protesters opposing immigration-related arrests in the neighbourhood.

US District Judge Sara Ellis said in court on Tuesday, according to a CBS News report: “Those kids were tear gassed on their way to celebrate Halloween in their local school parking lot,” and “I can only imagine how terrifi ed they were.”

Ellis was questioning US Customs and Border Patrol Commander Gregory Bovino over incidents that protesters allege violate a temporary restraining order (TRO) she issued earlier this month.

The Oct. 9 TRO was issued after a group of journalists, faith leaders and protesters filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration.

The lawsuit described a “pattern of extreme brutality” by federal agents against peaceful protesters since Operation Midway Blitz began on Sept. 2.

Operation Midway Blitz is described as a multi-agency operation against “criminal illegal aliens” in Illinois.

A subsequent court filing on Oct. 27 alleges that federal agents have violated the TRO “almost every day” since it was issued.

The filing stated: “Immigration enforcement does not typically require the daily use of tear gas on civilians in residential areas.”

Bellingcat’s analysis of social media videos from 28 events in Illinois from Oct. 9 to Oct. 27 found multiple examples of force and riot control weapons being used.

Seven events appeared to show the use of riot control weapons when there was seemingly no apparent immediate threat by protesters and no audible warnings given.

Nineteen events showed use of force, such as tackling people to the ground when they were not visibly resisting.

Another seven events showed agents ordering or threatening people to leave public places.

Each of the events included in the dataset were verified to have taken place in Illinois in recent weeks with at least two sources.

The sources included videos taken by different people, local reporting, or statements from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

The DHS oversees Customs and Border Patrol (CBP) and the United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin said DHS officers “only use crowd control methods as a last resort when repeated warnings have been given”.

DHS said its officers were “facing a 1,000 percent increase in assaults against them”.

The court order places restrictions on federal agents’ crowd-control measures within the state.

The court order includes “riot control weapons” such as tear gas and pepper spray.

The court order restricts the use of force against individuals.

The court order restricts requiring people to leave public spaces that they lawfully have the right to be in.

The TRO says riot control weapons or force may be deployed where there is an “immediate threat” of physical harm to the agents or others.

The TRO says riot control weapons or force may be deployed when multiple warnings have been given.

The TRO says riot control weapons or force may be deployed where “necessary and proportional” for an arrest.

The TRO prohibits the use of riot control weapons on people “who are not posing an immediate threat to the safety of a law enforcement off icer or others”.

The TRO prohibits using riot control weapons where using them on intended targets would result in injury to others who are not posing any threat.

The TRO prohibits federal agents from “issuing a crowd dispersal order requiring any person to leave a public place that they lawfully have a right to be, unless dispersal is justifi ed by exigent circumstances as defi ned by Department of Homeland Security Use of Force Policy”.
