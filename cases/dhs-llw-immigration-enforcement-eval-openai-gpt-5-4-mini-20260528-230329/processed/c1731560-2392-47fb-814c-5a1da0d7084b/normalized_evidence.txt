Five Shots in Five Minutes: Analysing One Federal Agent’s Use of Less-Lethal Launcher in Minneapolis

The fatal shooting of Renee Nicole Good by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent Jonathan Ross in Minneapolis on Jan. 7 sparked nationwide protests.

An elite Border Patrol officer, masked but identifiable through the uniform number patch EZ-17, was captured on camera firing his B&T GL06 40mm less-lethal launcher at protesters five times in five minutes.

EZ-17 fired the launcher while travelling down a street adjacent to where Good was killed.

Experts told Bellingcat the agent’s use of the less-lethal launcher was “punitive” and “questionable at best”.

Four of the shots appeared to be aimed directly at protesters’ faces at close range.

A fifth shot was fired from a distance towards a crowd after tear gas had already been deployed.

A sixth shot, captured at another location on the same day, shows EZ-17 firing a shot from the same launcher at someone at head-level.

In footage captured by independent news outlet Mercado Media, EZ-17 is seen inside the crime scene tape perimeter, standing near Border Patrol Commander Gregory Bovino, with eight 40mm munitions on his belt.

These included three sponge-nose impact rounds.

These included five cylindrical munitions that can be filled with different payloads and chemical irritants.

“BLAST” in blue text is visible on one munition.

“BLAST” in blue text indicates a “Muzzle Blast” munition with a CS gas fill.

At least three additional 40mm munitions are visible in EZ-17’s plate carrier.

Another video by independent news network Status Coup News showed uninterrupted footage capturing five shots from the time the agent exited the crime scene perimeter at 5:02 to when he left in a truck with other agents at 9:23.

The back of EZ-17’s vest shows that he belongs to CBP’s Border Patrol Tactical Unit (BORTAC).

Members of BORTAC have regularly accompanied Bovino as he leads Trump’s immigration raids.

EZ-17 and EZ-2 have continued to accompany Bovino on raids in Minnesota in subsequent days.
